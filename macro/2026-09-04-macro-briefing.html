A 162k print on a 31k trend

August payrolls tripled consensus and the market immediately re-priced a September hike. The composition says the labour market is steadier than it is hot — but with the Fed in blackout from tomorrow, next Friday’s CPI is the only vote that still counts.

S&P 500 7,718.60 −0.38% · −29.11
Sept hike priced 58.4% +9.0 pts on the day
Brent crude $95.23 ≈+8% on the week
The read

Two forces are pulling the front end the same way, and only one of them is really about growth. Payrolls beat by roughly 3×, but 104k of the 162k came from leisure & hospitality and local-government education — both rebounds from prior declines, both mean-reverting. Strip them and you are near the 31k twelve-month trend. This is a report that repairs July’s −23k, not one that reopens a wage spiral.

The second force is the barrel. Brent up ~8% on the week and ~20% on the month on Hormuz risk is a supply shock, and supply shocks are exactly what a hawkish chair points at when arguing that inflation expectations need defending. That is the transmission channel to watch, not payrolls.

Net: I would fade the idea that today’s print settled the September question. It moved the odds to a coin-flip-plus; the 11 September CPI decides it.

The arithmetic that matters: those two lines are 104,000 of the 162,000. Both are reversals of prior weakness rather than fresh demand, and local-government education is one of the most seasonal-adjustment-sensitive series in the report. Ex those categories the month lands near 58,000 — above the 31,000 trend, but nowhere near a print that forces a hand.

The honest hawkish counter is that the unemployment rate did not budge at 4.1% despite a labour force that has been growing. A steady rate on a rising participation base is genuine absorption, and it is the piece of this report that survives the composition critique.

03 The path FOMC 15–16 September

Fed funds futures moved to a 58.4% probability of a September hike, up 9.0 percentage points on the day. That is the market’s first sustained majority for tightening in this cycle’s turn, and it sits on top of a hawkish Jackson Hole from Chair Warsh on 28 August.

Cutting the other way: Governor Waller said on 3 September he would be inclined to support holding at the current setting if the disinflation in the next fortnight’s data holds — explicitly making August CPI the swing input for his vote. He also flagged a pending Commerce methodology change to how financial-services fees are estimated, which he expects could lower twelve-month PCE inflation by a few tenths, and noted that non-market services were roughly half of July’s core PCE increase. Read plainly: a senior governor is arguing the inflation data overstates the underlying rate, days before the print that decides the meeting.

So the committee arrives at 16 September with a chair leaning hawkish, at least one governor leaning hold, and a single CPI release between them. That is an unusually binary setup for a meeting priced at 58/42, and it argues for owning optionality rather than direction into 11 September.

04 The barrel Strait of Hormuz

The US struck Iran again this week after roughly a month of calm; Tehran retaliated against American bases in the region and against shipping in the strait. Transit volume is the number to track, not the headline: six commodity vessels crossed on Wednesday, against eleven on Tuesday and a ten-day average near thirteen. That is a better-than-50% drop in throughput in a single session.

Brent near $95 already embeds a fear premium, but a premium priced off disrupted transit is unstable in both directions — it decays fast if vessels return, and it gaps if they do not. For a portfolio, the asymmetry is that this is the one live channel that can turn a hold into a hike by way of headline CPI and inflation expectations, without any change in the labour data at all.

06 What would change the view

  • Core CPI at or above 0.4% m/m on 11 September. That takes the hike from contested to consensus and puts the 2-yr through 4.50%. The composition argument on payrolls stops mattering.
  • Hormuz transits back above ten a day. The fear premium in Brent unwinds quickly, headline inflation risk drops out, and Waller’s hold case becomes the base case.
  • Gold turning higher with crude. The one combination that says the market has stopped pricing a supply shock and started pricing a policy error. Currently absent — gold sits 21% below its January high.
  • September payrolls reverting toward trend. If the leisure and education rebounds give back next month, the 31k trend reasserts and this week’s repricing looks like an overreaction to a single noisy print.
