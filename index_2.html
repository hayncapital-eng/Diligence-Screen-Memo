Manager Diligence · Phase 1 Quantitative Screen

Skill or Luck? Testing a Short, High-Volatility Track Record

Brayden Kai Miyahara  ·  August 2026

Disclosure: the manager under review is the author. A personal discretionary account, highly concentrated. Long-only equity through 2024; multi-instrument from 2025. 30 monthly observations, March 2024 – August 2026.

Recommendation: Decline to allocate

Cumulative return of +760% over 30 months, ending 21.7% below its high-water mark. Two findings decide it, and neither depends on the other:

  • Capacity. The largest contributor to the record, 16.2% of it, traded $685,000 a day in 2024. At a $25m ticket that one position would have taken 109 trading days to exit.
  • Alpha. Jensen’s alpha against MSCI ACWI is +4.06%/mo at t 1.09, not significant. Proving it would take 87 to 100 months. Thirty exist.

  • Channel one, the beta. Portfolio volatility (23.15%/mo) and mean excess return (9.24%) are properties of the book, not of the benchmark. A benchmark a quarter as volatile needs a vol ratio nearly four times larger, and even with a lower correlation the beta still triples, so it absorbs more of the fixed 9.24%.
  • Channel two, the residual. The market also explains less of the variance, so the residual widens and the standard error with it. Together the two channels take t from 1.46 to 1.09: 36% the smaller alpha, 64% the wider error.
  • Drawdown against the same market. Under water 15 of 30 months against 9, and six times deeper: 38.6% against 6.1% for ACWI.
What would resolve it is a longer record. n = (2 × residual SD ÷ alpha)2 gives 87 months; requiring t to reach 2.00 gives 100. Both are totals, and thirty exist, so another 57 to 70: five to six more years of identical performance against the market the fund already owns. A 760% return is not “proven edge” but insufficient sample.

  • The Sharpe clears, but the distribution is not normal. Nineteen of 122 positions produced ~97% of the equity book, the three largest months are 42% of all positive return, and the only two observations beyond two standard deviations are both on the upside.
  • Skew +0.99. Using Bailey–López de Prado to adjust for the positive skew lifts the Sharpe t to 2.54 from 2.19. The adjustment flatters this record rather than testing it.
  • MSCI ACWI’s Sharpe over the same period is 1.31. The past thirty months have been a bull market, lifting equities well above their long-run Sharpe. The excess over owning the index is 0.08.

  • It clears against the market and fails against the sector, on t-statistics of 2.06 and 1.97.
  • And the information ratio assumes a beta near one. At beta 4.34 to the market, active return is mostly leverage rather than selection, so an IR of 1.30 flatters this record the same way the skew adjustment flatters the Sharpe.

Every measure that clears is one that ignores how much market exposure produced the return. The Sharpe uses no benchmark at all. The information ratio subtracts the benchmark once. Jensen’s alpha subtracts it 4.34 times, and that is the one that fails. The verdict turns on whether you adjust for beta, and an endowment sizing this against a beta budget has to.

05What this screen cannot establish

Returns-based analysis is one workstream of six. Remaining diligence, in priority order:

  • Holdings-based attribution: allocation versus selection, which returns-based work cannot separate
  • Net-of-fee alpha: gross throughout; net of 2/20 the SOXX alpha falls from 4.50% to 3.72%/mo
  • Process, operations and key-person risk: sources of edge; custody and valuation of illiquid marks; single decision-maker
  • Peer percentile: no manager universe to rank against
  • Independent verification: returns are self-exported broker statements, neither audited nor GIPS-verified

06What would change the recommendation

  • Stock picking that works outside AI and semiconductors. Within the equity book, 103 of 122 positions contributed 2.8% between them; nineteen names produced the other 97%
  • The same process, and the same results, in more liquid securities.
  • Performance through a falling market. The worst peak-to-trough in ACWI across these 30 months is 6.1%. This process has never been observed in a broad-market drawdown
